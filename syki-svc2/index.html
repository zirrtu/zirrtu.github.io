SYKI-SVC2: Separating Who Sings from How It Sounds via Dual-Reference Zero-Shot Singing Voice Conversion

SVC Fixed cross-singer pairs Dry and wet B

Abstract

Zero-shot singing voice conversion (SVC) transforms source singing to match an unseen target singer’s timbre while preserving content information. However, existing systems rely on a single timbre reference prompt. In practice, users rarely have studio-grade recording environments or equipment; even with front-end denoising or conservative training perturbation, low-quality prompts severely degrade output quality and speaker similarity. We propose SYKI-SVC2, a dual-reference zero-shot SVC framework built on a Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) Transformer, where the source provides content information, prompt A specifies singer identity, and prompt B specifies recording acoustics. Thus, pairing a degraded prompt A with any clean studio prompt B stabilizes and restores output quality. It employs two encoders: an identity encoder S combining frozen CAM++ with a residual Transformer head, trained under acoustic degradations to focus on vocal timbre rather than environment or quality; and an effect encoder B extracting global and multi-scale temporal tokens injected via AdaLN and cross-attention, trained with a singer adversary to focus on environment and quality rather than identity. On clean SVC, SYKI-SVC2 stays comparable to Seed-VC; under degraded prompts, it achieves state-of-the-art objective scores— SS 0.5855 vs. 0.5598, SIG 3.4838, and PAM 0.9297—and human SMOS/QMOS of 3.51/3.81 vs. 2.73/2.88. Ablations verify our design.
